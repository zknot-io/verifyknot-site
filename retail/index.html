The problem

Every record in a dispute has the same author

When a buyer says it arrived opened, or that it is not the item in the photos, the evidence on your side is a photograph you took and a tracking number from a carrier. Both were produced by the party being asked about. There is nothing an outsider can check, so the dispute is settled by platform policy — which is to say, by whichever of you is more expensive to lose.

A named device confirmed something specific about a specific parcel at a specific time, and your buyer can check it without trusting either of us.

That sentence is the whole offer. If a claim does not fit inside it, we do not make it.

Exactly how it works

Your side — about 90 seconds per order

  1. Pack and photograph the item.

    Nothing about how you work changes here.

  2. Write the one line you would stand behind in a dispute.

    “Graded PSA 9, cert 88412093, no case damage, packed by SW.” Put it in a file — a note, the packing slip, or the photo itself.

  3. Close the box and apply a seal across the closure.

    Destructive-open construction: it has to be destroyed to get in. Each seal is individually serialized and carries a QR to its own public record. Record the seal code against the order.

  4. Sign the file with your device.

    Open verifyknot.io/sign, drop the file, connect over USB, press the button. The file is hashed in your browser and never uploaded — we receive a fingerprint, not your contents. You get a short code back.

  5. Put both codes on the packing slip and in the shipping email.

    The seal code answers was it opened? The signing code answers what did they say was in it?

Their side — about 15 seconds, no account

Before opening

They scan the seal. It resolves to a public record showing that this seal was registered, and when. If the seal is voided, they can see that before they touch anything.

After opening

They open the signing code. The signature is re-verified in their browser, against the record's own data — not against a service that might one day answer differently.

If they want to be sure

They drop your condition file into the same page. Its SHA-256 either matches the one that was signed or it does not. One changed byte and it does not.

Months later — the loop that pays for the other two

An investigator, a platform reviewer, a chargeback team or a court reads the same public record without either party's cooperation and without an account. The lookup endpoint is unauthenticated by design. That is the point of the whole thing: the record outlives the argument, and it does not need you to be around.

The seals

2×1 in destructive-open polyester. Passive — no electronics, no battery, nothing to fail. Each one is individually serialized and registered before it ships. You can put three lines of your own text on them (store name up to 16 characters, two more lines up to 22), printed in a zone kept visually separate from ours so it is never mistaken for something we are vouching for. We do not store that text.

The limits, stated plainly

What this does not do

It does not prove your claim was true
It proves what was claimed, by which device, at what time, and that the seal was intact. Whether the item matched the description is still a human judgement. What changes is that the argument moves from whose word to whose record.
A seal makes no claim about contents
It is tamper-evident, never tamper-proof. It records that a serialized seal was registered, and its own destruction is visible. It says nothing about what was behind it.
Registration vouches for a key, not for a machine
On the KEY-REGISTERED rung the key is on file and was generated inside a secure element it has never left — but the device around it is not attested, and a modified unit signs with the same registered key. The demo above prints this in the rail's own words rather than ours.
We do not yet publish our registry key where you can check it
Half of what you saw above is independent: your browser re-verified the signature against the key carried in the record. Whether that key is one ZKNOT vouches for is currently reported by ZKNOT. Until we publish it out-of-band, treat that half as our assertion. We would rather say so than let it read as grounding it is not.
Records are tamper-evident, not immutable
The chain is hash-linked and served publicly, so alteration is detectable. Nobody should tell you it is impossible.
Signing needs a desktop
Web Serial is desktop Chrome, Edge or Opera. Your buyer verifies on anything; you sign at a computer. This is a high-value, low-volume motion today.
